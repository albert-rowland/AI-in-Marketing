# Home Coffee Brewing Guide

## Page 1 · Home Coffee Brewing Guide

A repeatable starting point for your next cup.

Choose one brewing method, record your recipe, and adjust one variable at a time. This Copper Cup guide supports a fictional marketing campaign. Its brewing suggestions are starting points that vary with equipment and taste.

## Page 2 · Your brewing setup

A method you can repeat.

Choose a brewer you already own and follow its manufacturer instructions. A scale helps you record coffee and water weights. Keep a timer and a notebook nearby. Write your brewer, filter, coffee, and grind setting on the worksheet before comparing recipes.

## Page 3 · Coffee and water ratio

A starting recipe for pour-over.

Stumptown suggests approximately one part coffee to 17 parts water by weight as a general pour-over starting point. For this worksheet, 20 g coffee pairs with 340 g water. The ratio is a starting recipe, with taste and equipment guiding your adjustments.

## Page 4 · Preparing a pour-over

Preparation helps repeat your recipe.

Rinse the paper filter with hot water and discard the rinse water. Grind close to brewing time and record the setting. Stumptown describes a bloom pour that wets the grounds before the remaining water. Consult its full guide for the selected brewer and recipe.

## Page 5 · A French press starting point

Follow a method-specific recipe.

Blue Bottle describes a coarse grind, 30 g coffee, 350 g water, and a four-minute brew for its French press method. Pour the brewed coffee into another vessel after brewing. Use that published recipe as a reference and adjust through recorded trials.

## Page 6 · Your first tasting notes

Describe the cup before changing it.

Record the recipe, brew time, and your taste impression. Compare sweetness, bitterness, body, and the overall preference in your own words. Repeat the baseline once before making a change so your notes show a consistent comparison.

## Page 7 · A troubleshooting worksheet

One change creates a clearer comparison.

Stumptown suggests a finer grind when the cup tastes weak or sour and a coarser grind when it tastes too bitter. Treat that advice as a starting hypothesis. Record the problem, one proposed change, the result, and the next adjustment.

## Page 8 · Your three-cup comparison

Keep the recipe consistent.

Use one brewer and the recorded recipe for each roast. Give each cup a label and write your preference before comparing notes. This worksheet helps you describe a choice rather than assign an objective quality score.

## Page 9 · Your next brewing session

Choose one repeatable routine.

Write the brewer, coffee dose, water dose, grind setting, and one adjustment for your next session. Keep the recipe you prefer and note any equipment or coffee change before comparing another cup.
